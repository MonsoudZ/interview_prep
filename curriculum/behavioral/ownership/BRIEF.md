# Ownership beyond the ticket

Tell me about an important problem that had no clear owner. How did you decide to take responsibility, bring others with you, and know when your involvement should end?

## Your response
Use a real example you are comfortable discussing. Explain the situation and constraints, your responsibility, the actions you personally took, what changed, and what you learned. A two-minute opening answer is a useful practice goal; the eight-minute stage also leaves room to refine and reflect. This is typed rehearsal; you can speak aloud alongside it, but no audio is recorded or assessed.

## Evidence and boundaries
Separate your work from the team’s work. State the limits of your recollection. If exact metrics are unavailable, describe an observable outcome and how you learned about it. Do not invent numbers, outcomes, or personal experience. An unsuccessful outcome can still support a thoughtful example. Omit confidential names, customer data, and sensitive details.

## Practice modes
Guided practice supports progressive hints and a frozen story-bank copy in preparation.md. Write the actual response in answer.md. A standalone timed attempt starts fresh without importing a story; its timer continues if you leave the page. Existing preparation is not proof of an unfamiliar interview performance.

## Review
Self-review considers clarity, personal contribution, this theme’s judgment, and honest reflection. Cite your submitted answer, not just preparation notes. Ratings describe this response; they do not establish truthfulness, personality, hiring suitability or how you sounded aloud. After submission, rehearse the published follow-up probes.
